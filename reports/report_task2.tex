\section{Optical depth}

The equation for optical depth for vertical incidence where dz goes from the bottom of the atmosphere upwards:
\begin{equation}
\tau(z,\lambda, 0) = \sum_{j} \sigma_{j}^{a}(\lambda) \int_{z}^{\infty} n_j(z') \,dz'
\end{equation}
With adding the geometry for non-vertical incidence this is for some angles up to about 60\si{\degree}:
\begin{equation}
\tau(z,\lambda, \chi) =\frac{1}{cos \chi} \sum_{j} \sigma_{j}^{a}(\lambda) \int_{z}^{\infty} n_j(z') \,dz'
\end{equation}
This gets more complicated for even higher incidence angles up to 90\si{\degree}. 
\begin{equation}
\tau(z,\lambda, \chi) = \sum_{j} \sigma_{j}^{a}(\lambda) \int_{z}^{\infty} n_j(z') \left[1 -  \left( \frac{R_E + z_0}{R_E + z'} \right)^2 sin^2 \chi_0 \right]^{-1/2}  \,dz'
\end{equation}
For my calculations I used equation (2), as this reduces to equation(1) for vertical incidence anyways as cos(0)=1. 

But first the cross sections had to be interpolated to the wavelength resolution in the irradiance file. I used np.interpolate as this gave the best fiting results. But I get 0 for all wavelength after 100nm as there is no more data in the cross section file. Because of that I only used wavelengths in the interval [0,100]nm in the calculations and dropped the rest due to insufficient data. I also converted all data to SI units for the calculations, I also early on converted energy flux in $Watts/m^2$ into photonflux in $\frac{photons}{s \cdot m^2}$. As photons at different wavelength have different energies I wrote a function that converts energy into number of photons dependent on the wavelength:
\begin{python}
##convert Watts/m^2 into photons/s/m^2 (Plancks law E= hc/wavelength)
def watts2photons(E_total, wavelengths):
    h = 6.62607015e-34 #plancks constant
    c = 2.99792458e8 #speed of light [m/s]
    E_i = h*c/np.array(wavelengths)
    nr_photons = E_total/E_i
    return nr_photons
\end{python}

EUV fluxes for different heights and wavelengths can be calculated from the optical depth as:
\begin{equation}
I(z,\lambda) = I^{\infty}(\lambda)exp(-\tau(z,\lambda,\chi))
\end{equation}
$I^{\infty}(\lambda)$ is the photon flux at infinity, or at the top of the thermosphere.

photo ionization profiles as function of altitude

\begin{equation}
q_j^i(z,l) = n_j(z) \int_{0}^{\lambda_{th}}I(z,\lambda)\sigma_j^{i,j}(\lambda)d\lambda
\end{equation}

where $\sigma_j^{i,j}$ denotes the ionization cross-sections and I the EUV fluxes as calculated in task 2. 


equation for chapman profiles (see Brekke equ. 4.21):
\begin{equation}
q = q_{m,0}exp\bigl[1-\frac{z-z_{m,0}}{H}-sec\chi exp\bigl(-\frac{z-z_{m,0}}{H}\bigr)\bigr]
\end{equation}
$q_{m,0}$ is the ion production rate at its maximum for an overhead sun (SZA=0) and $z_{m,0}$ is the height of maximum production for SZA=0.
